The 122 outside our nomination-paper collection

602 CTAs = 480 present in the 15 registered nomination papers + 122 absent from that collection. The latter represent 123 loci, because PRAMEF25/6 shares the selected reference protein. “Not in our collection” does not mean “never published,” “novel,” or “unsupported.”

What do they have in common?

The clearest difference is their route into the classification: 60 of 122 use developmental evidence alone, compared with 34 of 480 in the registered collection. Another 49 use adult reproductive evidence alone and 13 use both. These are dataset-derived admission routes, not exclusive biological identities. WEE2, for example, enters through developmental measurements here despite direct human oocyte evidence.

The missing literature includes early embryo gene activation, female germ cells and maternal-effect biology, epididymal secretions and reproductive-organ proteins. These deserve separate evidence labels. An embryo-associated protein is not necessarily fetal throughout gestation; an epididymal secretory protein is not necessarily made by germ cells. Association with development also does not automatically justify calling something a demonstrated oncofetal antigen.

Are they weaker?

Not uniformly. The median of each protein’s highest stored tumor RNA is similar in the two sets. Maxima are sensitive to sampled tumor types and outliers; they do not measure prevalence, reproducibility or tumor-to-normal selectivity. All 122 pass the released coding-translation rule, which accepts qualifying reference annotation. That is different from experimental tumor protein detection.

Weak points remain: cancer presence requires only one positive RNA sample, developmental concentration lacks a comparable reference panel, normal-state coverage is incomplete, and all 122 lack entries in the released gene-knowledge findings ledger. That empty ledger means no findings recorded there, not that each protein received a negative literature review. The stored replication flag was assessed only in a selected subset and cannot support a fair whole-set comparison.

Why were they overlooked?

There are several distinguishable explanations: missing cancer-target publications, source lists focused on testis rather than embryos or female reproduction, changing names or gene models, and evidence that concerns a fusion rather than the reference protein. These are demonstrated examples and plausible explanations, not proven reasons for every omission. The current data also admit some proteins with normal somatic functions that need focused review.

What the focused literature review found

What should change in the next evidence release?

  1. Resolve the normal-cell findings first, especially sensory neurons and bone-forming cells. Record exact human tissue, state, identity and the reason for retaining or changing each call.
  2. Add missing cancer-target nominations, with exact protein or locus mapping. A nomination supplies provenance; it does not bypass the normal-tissue gates.
  3. Expand the developmental, oocyte, maternal-effect and reproductive-secretion bibliography. Store these as distinct evidence types rather than relabeling every biology paper as a CTA nomination.
  4. Review endogenous tumor translation, prevalence and unique peptide evidence, particularly for duplicated genes, historical pseudogene annotations and fusion-associated proteins.
  5. Replay the full inventory and publish the changed calls with their reasons. Freeze this 602/480/122 comparison as the historical baseline.

RNA limits in plain language

Without usable matching protein staining, the starting upper RNA limits are 4 for Vital, 8 for Protected and 16 for Manageable, in the source’s own TPM or nTPM units. Matching negative immunohistochemistry (IHC) can raise these limits:

The upper limit is inclusive: a value must be above it to trigger this RNA rule. A Vital/Protected exceedance supplies Unsafe evidence. A Manageable exceedance blocks CTA admission; a value above 128 supplies Unsafe evidence. Reviewed product-specific clinical credit can add one doubling within the same cap. These numbers are policy choices, not experimentally established safe exposure levels. Corneal CP10K uses separately calibrated limits divided by 100; this is not a TPM conversion. Reproductive and Disposable compartments have separate rules.

Data sources and what they contribute

Source names in the candidate table and gate rules link here. Measurement datasets, nomination papers, curated findings and computed rules serve different purposes. The two rare-cell gates use the CELLxGENE panel. Sequence containment is computed from Ensembl coding sequences and released Unsafe assignments. Final classification combines the gates; it is not another measurement.

Why protect these tissues?

Vital, Protected, Manageable and Disposable are OncoRef policy categories, not published, calibrated probabilities of fatal CAR-T or TCR-T toxicity. The registry distinguishes direct human injury, animal depletion, regenerative experiments, clinical analogies and anatomical inference. A citation supports the stated mechanism and scope; it does not validate every numerical RNA limit.

Whole lung is Vital; mature ciliated airway cells are Protected in the current registry. Their mucociliary role, replacement and injury evidence are assessed separately from alveolar gas exchange and progenitor loss.
