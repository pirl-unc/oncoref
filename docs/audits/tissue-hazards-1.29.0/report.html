What normal-tissue injury can cost

107 curated rules; 50 references; 123 human anatomy concepts; 1072 native mapping rows.

This ontology assigns adult human compartments to four protection priorities. It separates immediate functional loss, persistent injury, regenerative capacity and available compensation. The assignments are policy judgments supported by different kinds of evidence, not measured probabilities of fatal toxicity.

The initiating target, inflammatory amplifiers and the tissue causing final organ failure can be different. A preserved stem-cell pool does not ensure recovery under persistent recognition. Conversely, the label 'stem cell' alone does not prove irreplaceability: alternative progenitors sometimes compensate.

Reproductive/developmental membership remains an independent anatomical axis. RNA detection, accessible surface antigen, peptide-HLA presentation, functional recognition and demonstrated tissue injury are distinct evidence states. This registry classifies the consequence of injury; it does not manufacture missing recognition evidence. Adult human calls are not validated animal or pediatric tolerability claims.

How the ontology works

Bulk measurements use organ-level rules. A narrower exception requires a resolved tissue and cell identity from a cellular assay. Unknown cell labels inherit organ concern; mixed labels cannot earn a downgrade. Unknown anatomy, conflicting rules and contexts outside adult human scope remain unresolved.

The RNA and IHC cascade reads these same assignments. Existing numeric RNA/IHC ceilings are research parameters, not newly calibrated by the literature review. In the existing brain/spinal-cord/heart/lung rare-cell panel, qualifying Vital findings retain the Unsafe screen, while Protected findings create a candidate review hold. The 1% detection line is a screening parameter, not a risk estimate. Other organs have ontology entries without an implied new rare-cell data panel.

Disposable is a narrow conditional allowance for specified compartments. A removable organ does not make its widely shared cell types disposable elsewhere. The existing reproductive permission remains separate from the functional hazard reported here. Historical reports retain their original versioned policy.
