What do they have in common?

60 of the 122 qualify through developmental evidence alone, compared with 34 of the 480. Another 49 qualify through adult reproductive evidence alone and 13 through both. The biological categories below distinguish early embryos, oocytes and maternal function, reproductive secretions, testis, placenta and gastrulation.

Are they weaker?

The median maximum tumor RNA is 35.6 TPM for the 122 and 33.5 TPM for the 480. Maximum tumor RNA is below 10 TPM for 23 of 122 and 80 of 480. All 122 pass the reference-coding translation rule. Their released gene-knowledge ledger contains no findings.

These comparisons use each protein’s highest measured tumor RNA. Tumor prevalence and experimental protein detection are separate measurements. The replication flag was calculated for a selected subset, so it is omitted from this comparison.

Biological categories of the 602 CTAs

Proteins can have several categories. Each assignment records a publication or a measured tissue, cell population or developmental stage. The table shows the biological categories separately from the admission route and classification.

RNA tags use supported values of at least 5 TPM or nTPM. Oocyte tags use oocyte-specific observations; reproductive-secretion tags use publications. Testis bulk RNA receives the testis category. Published animal findings retain their species. Categories overlap, so their counts are not added.

RNA limits in plain language

Without usable matching protein staining, the starting upper RNA limits are 4 for Vital, 8 for Protected and 16 for Manageable, in the source’s own TPM or nTPM units. Matching negative immunohistochemistry (IHC) can raise these limits:

Vital481632

Manageable163264128

The upper limit is inclusive: a value must be above it to trigger this RNA rule. A Vital/Protected exceedance supplies Unsafe evidence. A Manageable exceedance blocks CTA admission; a value above 128 supplies Unsafe evidence. Reviewed product-specific clinical credit can add one doubling within the same cap. These are OncoRef screening thresholds. Corneal CP10K uses separate limits equal to these values divided by 100. Reproductive and Disposable compartments have separate rules.

Data sources and what they contribute

Source names in the candidate table and gate rules link here. Measurement datasets, nomination papers, curated findings and computed rules serve different purposes. The two rare-cell gates use the CELLxGENE panel. Sequence containment is computed from Ensembl coding sequences and released Unsafe assignments. Final classification combines the gates; it is not another measurement.

Why protect these tissues?

Vital, Protected, Manageable and Disposable are OncoRef tissue-protection categories. The registry records human injury, animal depletion, regeneration, treatment and anatomical evidence for each assignment. The RNA thresholds are defined by OncoRef policy.

Whole lung is Vital; mature ciliated airway cells are Protected in the current registry. Their mucociliary role, replacement and injury evidence are assessed separately from alveolar gas exchange and progenitor loss.
